01 Section 1 overview & pedagogical philosophy

Why Initiative-Informed Planning Matters

Traditional school-based health education often relies on isolated, top-down instructional units or reactive zero-tolerance discipline. However, evidence demonstrates that fragmented interventions fail to alter complex health behaviours such as adolescent e-cigarette use (vaping) because they ignore the broader social, cultural, and environmental determinants of health (Victorian Department of Health [VDH], 2023; World Health Organisation [WHO], 2021).

To achieve sustained, positive health and educational outcomes, health promotion must be grounded in systemic, evidence-based educational initiatives. Aligned with the Framework for Improving Student Outcomes 2.0 (FISO 2.0; Department of Education, 2024) and the Victorian Healthy Places Framework for Schools (Healthy Places Healthy Futures, n.d.), Project Unclouded integrates three foundational pillars:

1

Student Voice, Agency, and Partnership

Moving from passive instruction to genuine power-sharing and student-led co-design (Hunter, 2023).

2

High Impact Wellbeing Strategies (HIWS)

Operationalising Strategy 5 (Fostering Student Self-Efficacy) and Strategy 3 (Creating Safe and Inclusive Environments) to build emotional regulation, resilience, and refusal mastery (Department of Education, 2021).

3

Community & Family Partnerships

Leveraging Bronfenbrenner’s ecological model and Indigenous Social and Emotional Wellbeing (SEWB) perspectives to create bi-directional engagement with Aurevian College's 53% Culturally and Linguistically Diverse (CALD) community and local health services.

1 Theoretical Rationale & Policy Context

Student voice is frequently misconstrued as simple consultation or tokenistic feedback collection. In contrast, Monash health education researcher Lisa Hunter (2023) conceptualises student voice along a Participation Continuum:

Low Agency

Doing to

Coercion, educating, and informing. Students are passive recipients of adult directives.

Medium Agency

Doing for

Consulting and engaging. Adults collect student opinions but retain all decision-making authority.

High Agency

Doing with

Co-designing, collaborating, and co-producing. Students and educators share power, agency, and responsibility.

When health initiatives operate at the "doing to" level—such as installing vape detectors or issuing punitive suspensions—they trigger student alienation, defiance, and secretiveness (Lockett et al., 2024). Adolescents strongly prefer health models grounded in peer connection, agency, and supportive relationships over adult surveillance (Lockett et al., 2024).

2 Practical Application in Project Unclouded

Project Unclouded operationalises high-agency participation by establishing a cohort of 15 Year 9–10 Wellbeing Champions. Rather than consuming pre-packaged anti-vaping flyers, these student leaders undergo structured co-design workshops (Module 2, Hours 5–7) to author digital media assets and run interactive stalls during the Unclouded Peer Festival.

Critical Nuance: Adult Safety Review Protocol

While empowering student voice is critical, unguided peer health messaging carries risks. Asdigian et al. (2023) demonstrated that unmonitored peer-led campaigns can accidentally cause social norm inflation (p = .002)—unintentionally exaggerating the perceived prevalence of vaping and making adolescents believe "everyone is doing it."

To mitigate this risk, Project Unclouded embeds an Adult Safety Review Protocol. Health and Physical Education (HPE) teachers and the Secondary School Nurse act as developmental mentors, validating student-created content against Australian Institute of Health and Welfare (AIHW, 2024) empirical prevalence data before public distribution.

1 Theoretical Rationale & Policy Context

The Victorian Department of Education’s High Impact Wellbeing Strategies (HIWS; 2021) provide a practical policy blueprint for integrating health and learning. Project Unclouded explicitly operationalises HIWS Strategy 5: Foster Student Self-Efficacy and HIWS Strategy 3: Create Safe and Inclusive Environments.

Albert Bandura’s (1977) Self-Efficacy Theory posits that an individual's belief in their capability to execute specific behaviours dictates whether they will initiate and sustain health-promoting actions. In adolescent vaping prevention, self-efficacy is the critical psychological mechanism required for a student to resist social pressure, refuse e-cigarettes, and seek help for nicotine dependency (Gardner et al., 2025).

2 Practical Application: Operationalising Bandura’s 4 Sources of Self-Efficacy

Project Unclouded explicitly embeds Bandura's four sources of self-efficacy across its 10-hour activity sequence:

Bandura's (1977) four sources of self-efficacy — program integration

1

Mastery Experiences (Performance Success)

Students practice social assertiveness and refusal scripts during Module 1 (Hour 3) HPE scenario role-plays.

2

Vicarious Experiences (Peer Modelling)

Younger students observe Year 9–10 Wellbeing Champions modelling healthy choices and running festival booths.

3

Verbal / Social Persuasion (Encouragement)

Secondary School Nurses and peers reinforce positive social norms (e.g., "Over 84% of local teens choose not to vape").

4

Emotional / Affective State Management

Creating a safe, non-punitive environment reduces peer anxiety and removes fear of suspension during help-seeking.

1 Theoretical Rationale & Policy Context

Adolescent wellbeing cannot be isolated within classroom walls. Grounded in Bronfenbrenner’s Ecological Systems Theory (1979) and the Indigenous Social and Emotional Wellbeing (SEWB) framework (Gee et al., 2014), health promotion must engage the entire ecosystem surrounding the young person—connecting the individual to family, school, health services, and broader cultural communities.

Whole-school partnership ecosystem (Bronfenbrenner, 1979)

MacrosystemCultural Ideologies, Public Health Laws (Tobacco Act 1987)
ExosystemVDH Policy, Headspace Melton, Local Government Area (LGA) Data
MesosystemHome-School Partnerships, CALD Community Networks, Nurse Pathways
MicrosystemAurevian College Classroom, Family Unit, Peer Group
Individual StudentHealth Literacy, Self-Efficacy, Agency

At Aurevian College, where 53% of students come from CALD backgrounds (specifically East African, South Sudanese, and South Asian cohorts), traditional one-way English notifications (e.g., punitive letters sent home regarding vaping infractions) erode trust and fail to foster health literacy (ABS, 2022). Effective partnerships require bi-directional communication—valuing cultural perspectives, addressing commercial determinants at home, and building a shared community identity around youth safety.

2 Practical Application in Project Unclouded

Bi-Lingual Family Health Literacy

Working alongside Aurevian’s CALD Liaison Officer, key health promotion materials, refusal strategies, and nicotine dependency information are translated into priority community languages (Swahili, Arabic, Dinka, Punjabi, Hindi).

Integrated Clinical Pathways

Establishing formal, non-punitive referral pathways with headspace Melton and the Secondary School Nursing Program. Students exhibiting severe nicotine dependency are offered voluntary, confidential cessation support rather than exclusionary discipline (Thomas et al., 2025).

Multi-Lingual Family Showcase (Hour 10)

The project culminates in an evening showcase where CALD families attend student-led stalls, breaking down language barriers and reinforcing health messaging at home.
